Introduction

Quantum error correction is a key ingredient of fault-tolerant quantum computation, protecting logical information from physical noise through redundant encoding and error recovery . Recent experiments demonstrate surface-code memories below threshold and elements of integrated fault-tolerant architectures . Their protection depends on the classical decoder used to interpret error syndromes, as improvements on experimental surface-code data illustrate . Scaling quantum error correction therefore requires accurate decoders with manageable computational cost.

Quantum degeneracy makes decoding more than error identification. Errors differing by a stabilizer act identically on encoded information; maximum-likelihood decoding therefore selects the logical class with the largest total probability . In an Ising formulation, even a globally minimum-energy error can belong to a less probable class. The challenge is to connect low-energy search to logical-class inference.

Several approaches address different parts of this problem. Minimum-weight perfect matching efficiently decodes suitable error graphs , while belief propagation with ordered-statistics post-processing handles more general parity-check structures . Tensor-network methods approximate class probabilities by contraction ; Markov-chain Monte Carlo, population annealing, and worm methods estimate probabilities or ratios through sampling . These approaches distinguish finding favorable configurations from estimating their class’s total weight.

Ising formulations connect quantum decoding to physics-inspired optimization. Digital Annealer decoding has been developed from quadratic unconstrained binary optimization and extended to circuit-level noise . Stabilizer-generator coordinates instead satisfy syndrome constraints exactly, as demonstrated for surface and color codes . Iterative low-order decoding reduces interaction order by approximating cross-type correlations through alternating subproblems . These approaches establish an optimization interface, while leaving the choice of search dynamics and the treatment of logical-class probabilities as distinct design questions.

The quantum inspiration for simulated bifurcation (SB) comes from dynamical approaches to optimization. Quantum annealing varies a Hamiltonian to seek low-energy configurations ; quantum bifurcation machines use the adiabatic evolution of Kerr-nonlinear parametric oscillators . SB replaces quantum-state evolution with classical position and momentum updates derived from the corresponding nonlinear dynamics , exemplifying physics-inspired computing on classical hardware. Synchronous SB updates support GPU and FPGA implementations, with favorable speed–quality tradeoffs on selected Ising benchmarks . Extensions include heating, bifurcation control, tabu search , higher-order objectives , and discrete-phase and Potts variables . These developments broaden the search procedure but do not themselves supply logical-class probabilities.

Combining search with class-probability estimation is also an active direction. Truncated-free-energy decoding reuses stochastic search candidates , while probabilistic formulations target class partition functions . We instead sum a complete prescribed generator-space neighborhood around one candidate per class, including configurations absent from the search output. At radius two, locality permits histogram-based counting without explicit pair enumeration.

We study the combination of constraint-preserving SB (CP-SB) and local stabilizer-orbit expansion (SOE), using the rotated surface code as a benchmark with an exact or tensor-network reference. Building on the established stabilizer-coordinate representation, our contributions are as follows:

- Complete local-neighborhood aggregation. Constraint-preserving SB (CP-SB) supplies class-specific candidates, and stabilizer-orbit expansion (SOE) sums a complete prescribed generator-space Hamming ball around each selected center, including unvisited configurations.

- Exact radius-two counting. An energy-change histogram and overlap corrections evaluate the local sum exactly. Counting takes linear work under bounded support and overlap degree once the center and support structure are available; this is not exact full-class inference.

- Controlled accuracy–resource comparisons. Held-out testing and paired exploratory budget studies separate search and aggregation effects. Matched-inference SB/SA comparisons identify settings with no significant accuracy difference but greater SB work and fewer sequential rounds, separately from implementation-specific timings.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section II constructs the decoder; Sec. III specifies evaluation and resource measures; Sec. IV reports accuracy, aggregation effects, and costs. Sections V and VI discuss limitations and conclude. Appendices A–C provide mathematical identities, reproducibility evidence, and circuit-level structural checks.

Decoding objective and logical classes

Decoding compares logical-class probabilities; search and aggregation approximate different parts of this task.

We consider independent depolarizing noise on a rotated planar surface code with distance d, n = d² data qubits, and m = n − 1 independent stabilizer generators. Each qubit experiences X, Y, or Z with probability p/3. For a binary symplectic error vector e ∈ 𝔽₂^(2n), let |e| count its nonidentity Pauli factors. Its probability is

$$P(e)=(1-p)^n w^{|e|},\qquad w=\frac{p}{3(1-p)}.
\tag{1}\label{eq:1}$$

The code encodes one logical qubit, giving four logical Pauli classes. We use the convention e = (e^(x), e^(z)), with Y represented by (1, 1) at a qubit but contributing only one unit to |e|. The measured syndrome is denoted by s.

Choose a syndrome-compatible seed e₀. Let G contain the independent stabilizer generators as rows, and let ℓ_(L) denote representatives of the four logical Pauli classes, L = 0, 1, 2, 3. Here G ∈ 𝔽₂^(m × 2n), all products and sums in Eq. (2) are over 𝔽₂, and the representatives ℓ_(L) are zero-syndrome logical operators. Every error in class L has a unique representation

e_(L)(u) = e₀ ⊕ ℓ_(L) ⊕ uG,   u ∈ 𝔽₂^(m).

The unnormalized class probability is

$$\begin{gathered}
Z_L(s)=\sum_u w^{|e_L(u)|}
=\sum_u e^{-\beta E_L(u)},\\
E_L(u)=|e_L(u)|,\quad \beta=\log\frac{3(1-p)}p.
\end{gathered}
\tag{3}\label{eq:3}$$

The conditional class probability is Z_(L)/∑_(L^(′))Z_(L^(′)). Maximum-likelihood decoding chooses the largest Z_(L), equivalently the smallest dimensionless free energy −β⁻¹log Z_(L) in the noise range studied. Here β is a statistical parameter fixed by the noise model, not a physical device temperature.

Candidate-MAP compares the lowest energies found in the classes; without exact search, it need not equal minimum-weight decoding. We instead use each candidate as the center of a prescribed local mass estimate.

Exact constraints and the Pauli-weight energy

Stabilizer coordinates enforce the constraints; the physical Pauli weight defines the search energy.

The seed is obtained by matching the X and Z components separately. A binary linear-system solution would also provide a feasible seed, although its quality can affect a finite-budget search. Equation (2) guarantees that each binary configuration preserves both syndrome and logical class.

With generator spins σ_(j) = (−1)^(u_(j)), define

$$\begin{gathered}
\tau_q^x=(-1)^{(e_0\oplus\ell_L)_q^x}
\prod_{j:G_{j,q}=1}\sigma_j,
\\
\tau_q^z=(-1)^{(e_0\oplus\ell_L)_q^z}
\prod_{j:G_{j,n+q}=1}\sigma_j.
\end{gathered}$$

The exact nonidentity indicator is

$$\mathbf1[e_q\ne I]
=\frac34-\frac14\tau_q^x-\frac14\tau_q^z
-\frac14\tau_q^x\tau_q^z.
\tag{4}\label{eq:4}$$

Summing Eq. (4) gives the Pauli-weight Hamiltonian. On this surface code each qubit touches at most two generators of each type, so the multilinear representation contains terms of order at most four. No syndrome penalty or auxiliary spin is required. The continuous SB variables are an optimization device; the associated sign configurations are the feasible physical errors. We use E_(L)(σ) for the multilinear spin representation, which agrees with E_(L)(u) at σ_(j) = (−1)^(u_(j)).

Constraint-preserving SB candidate search

With a feasible energy representation in place, we use SB to produce candidate centers for aggregation. The dynamics below determines how candidates are generated; their energies alone do not determine the final class decision.

For one replica, x, y ∈ ℝ^(m) denote positions and momenta. We set sgn (0) = +1 and recover generator bits through u_(j) = (1 − sgn x_(j))/2. For each class and restart, CP-SB evolves R replicas for S steps:

$$\begin{gathered}
y\leftarrow y+\Delta t[-(1-a_k)x+c f(\operatorname{sgn}x)],
\\ x\leftarrow x+\Delta t\,y,
\\ a_k=(k+1)/S.
\end{gathered}
\tag{5}\label{eq:5}$$

The step index is k = 0, …, S − 1: the momentum update uses the current positions, and the position update uses the newly updated momenta. After each update, a coordinate outside [−1, 1] is set to its boundary sign and its momentum is reset to zero. The initial momenta vanish. The force is the negative gradient of the multilinear energy evaluated at a sign vertex:

$$f_j(\sigma)=\frac{\sigma_j}{2}
\left[E_L(\sigma^{(j)})-E_L(\sigma)\right],
\tag{6}\label{eq:6}$$

where σ^((j)) flips spin j. This identity permits evaluation from physical generator flips without introducing a quadratic surrogate. The coupling is c = κ/max (1, max_(j)B_(j)), where B_(j) is the sum of absolute coefficients of energy monomials containing j.

Identical monomials are combined before this coefficient bound is evaluated.

Each class receives a warm restart with initial positions 0.08 + 𝒩(0, 0.01²) and a random restart with positions uniform in (−0.1, 0.1). Each restart has R replicas. Terminal sign configurations and the initial binary seed u = 0 form the candidate pool. Duplicate bitstrings are removed and the pool is sorted by energy, with lexicographic bitstring order breaking energy ties. The first candidate becomes the aggregation center. Intermediate trajectory states are not added to this pool. Our budget notation R × S therefore corresponds to 8R trajectories across four classes and two restart modes. The fixed search configuration is R = 8, S = 100, Δt = 0.45, and κ = 0.70.

Complete local-neighborhood aggregation

The selected centers provide a starting point for comparing classes, but each center represents only one error configuration. We therefore add the probability weights of a prescribed set of stabilizer-equivalent neighbors before making the decision.

The aggregation set is a complete, prescribed Hamming ball in generator coordinates, including configurations that the search did not visit. Its radius measures generator flips, not physical-error weight or spatial extent. For the selected center u_(L)^(*), define

$$\begin{gathered}
\widehat Z_L^{(r)}=
\sum_{d_H(u,u_L^*)\le r} w^{E_L(u)},
\\ \widehat L=\arg\max_L\widehat Z_L^{(r)}.
\end{gathered}
\tag{7}\label{eq:7}$$

The decoder returns e₀ ⊕ ℓ_(L̂) ⊕ u_(L̂)^(*)G as a correction. If several computed scores agree with the maximum within relative tolerance 10⁻¹⁵ (zero absolute tolerance), the implementation chooses the lowest class index. Independent generator rows ensure that every term represents a distinct physical error. Thus Eq. (7) is an exact sum over the specified ball and a lower bound on the full unnormalized class mass. It is not an exact class probability or a certificate for the winning class. Increasing the radius increases each mass for fixed centers, but does not guarantee a more accurate ordering of classes.

The radius-one and radius-two balls contain 1 + m and $1+m+\binom m2$ configurations. At d = 13, these counts are 169 and 14,197 per class.

Exact radius-two local-mass evaluation

The radius-two neighborhood contains quadratically many generator pairs, making explicit summation costly. We exploit additivity for disjoint supports and correct the overlapping pairs to compute the same local sum with less work.

Let Δ_(j) be the energy change from flipping generator j. For generators with disjoint physical supports,

Δ_(jk) = Δ_(j) + Δ_(k).

We first count pair sums using a convolution of the single-flip histogram, remove self-pairs, and divide the ordered-pair counts by two. We then replace the additive contribution of each overlapping pair by its true energy change. Let N_(t) count all radius-two configurations with energy change t, including the center, single flips, and corrected pairs. The resulting local mass is

Ẑ_(L)⁽²⁾ = ∑_(t)N_(t)w^(E_(L)(u_(L)^(*)) + t).

The implementation constructs integer counts, checks their nonnegativity and total $1+m+\binom m2$, and sums the corresponding probabilities in ascending Pauli-weight order. The counts and the prescribed local sum are exact mathematically; floating-point powers and summation introduce rounding. Exactness does not extend to the omitted part of the logical class. Appendix A gives the full counting identity.

With bounded generator support and bounded overlap degree, the local counting stage costs O(m) once the physical center and support structure are available. This complexity concerns the local sum. The archived implementation also reconstructs physical candidates with dense matrix operations and builds a cached overlap structure; its complete preprocessing and candidate-selection pipeline is not covered by that linear bound.

Matched simulated-annealing control

To assess what SB contributes to the decoder, we replace only the search procedure by simulated annealing while retaining the feasible representation and aggregation rule. This control separates the choice of search dynamics from the local probability estimator.

The CP-SA control shares the feasible seeds, class Hamiltonians, candidate-pool construction, and aggregation rule. It uses single-generator Metropolis proposals with a geometric temperature schedule from 2.0 to 0.05 over W sweeps, with C chains in each restart mode. Its budget is denoted C × W. Warm chains start at u = 0, while random chains start from independent uniform binary states. Each sweep visits a fresh random permutation of all m generators in each chain. Non-increasing proposals are accepted; an increase ΔE > 0 is accepted with probability e^(−ΔE/T). For W ≥ 2, the geometric schedule includes both endpoints; a single sweep uses the initial temperature. This search temperature is distinct from the channel parameter β in Eq. (3). Although the SA solver also records best-ever states, the benchmark prediction pool uses only terminal states and the seed, with the same deduplication and tie rules as SB. A sweep contains m sequential proposals per chain and is not equivalent in work to one SB step. Holding the inference radius fixed allows the two search procedures to be compared without changing the probability estimator.

Evaluation design

The evaluation separates three questions: how accurately the fixed-configuration decoder performs, how search and aggregation affect that accuracy, and what computational resources they require. We first test held-out errors after independent radius selection, then explore budgets and inference rules. Resource counts and implementation timings are reported separately.

Baselines and reference validation

Matching and correlated BP+OSD are accuracy baselines; CP-SA isolates search dynamics at fixed representation and inference; REF estimates logical-class decisions.

The matching baseline decodes the two Pauli components separately. Unit-weight matching and the tested probability-weighted PyMatching implementation produce identical grid predictions. The main BP baseline is correlated BP+OSD-CS with order seven, min-sum updates, and 3n iterations. Here H_(X) and H_(Z) are the binary X- and Z-stabilizer support matrices; H_(Z) detects the X component and H_(X) detects the Z component. Its binary check matrix is

$$\begin{pmatrix}H_Z&0&H_Z\\0&H_X&H_X\end{pmatrix},
\tag{9}\label{eq:9}$$

with X, Z, Y component priors p/3. The three binary blocks (x, z, y) map to physical components e^(x) = x ⊕ y and e^(z) = z ⊕ y. The implementation checks syndrome compatibility but imposes no explicit per-qubit exclusivity constraint on the three binary events. This construction is the specific correlated baseline tested here; it is not a claim about all BP-based decoders. It improves on the separately decoded BP+OSD baseline in all grid cells.

REF is exact class enumeration at d = 3. For d = 5, 7, 9, it uses an MPS decoder with bond dimension χ = 32. At d = 11, 13, it uses χ = 16, with χ = 32 replacements on selected difficult shots, capped at 3,000 per cell. Difficult shots were identified by a tested decoder failing or disagreeing with the χ = 16 prediction. When the difficult set exceeds the cap, 3,000 shots are selected by sampling without replacement with a fixed random state. This targeted check uses simulation information and is a reference-validation procedure, not a deployable decoder-selection rule.

On the checked difficult subsets at d ≥ 11, the χ = 16/32 comparison has at most two disagreements per cell, and the χ = 32/64 comparison has at most one. The latter check covers 18 cells with 401–3,000 shots each. These observations support a stable numerical reference on the tested subsets; they do not certify exact ML for every larger-code shot. Ratios below one on a finite sample need not imply a decoder with lower expected error than exact ML.

Statistical comparisons

Having specified the compared decoders, we distinguish uncertainty in each LER from uncertainty in their difference. Shared-shot, paired outcomes support the latter comparison.

LER intervals are Wilson 95% intervals. Paired differences use exact two-sided McNemar tests with Holm correction within each comparison family. Figure S2 instead shows conservative Clopper–Pearson difference intervals with Bonferroni allocation; these have at least 95% coverage under the binomial shot model but are not simultaneous Holm-adjusted intervals. Filled markers and interval crossings thus encode different procedures. Pooled discordant counts across cells are descriptive.

Holm families contain all 54 main-grid cells for each fixed decoder pair, including the different REF implementations. Budget families are specific to a configuration pair and inference rule over available cells: the three radius-two families in Fig. S2 contain 15, 18, and 18 cells. Extended-budget families contain 18 cells per decoder pair. Missing configurations are not imputed; correction does not cover all exploratory choices.

Each discordant-event probability receives a Clopper–Pearson interval with failure probability 0.025. Subtracting opposite endpoints gives an interval for LER(A) minus LER(B); negative values favor A.

Computational resources and timing

Accuracy alone does not determine computational usefulness. We therefore distinguish total search work, sequential depth, and measured CPU cost, defining the first two resources as

$$\begin{gathered}
N_{\Delta E}^{\rm SB}=8RSm,\quad
N_{\Delta E}^{\rm SA}=8CWm;\\
D_{\rm SB}=S,\quad D_{\rm SA}=Wm.
\end{gathered}
\tag{10}\label{eq:10}$$

The first counts single-generator flip-energy evaluations. The second counts update rounds when logical classes, restarts, and replicas or chains run in parallel, while SA updates within a chain remain sequential. These quantities exclude setup, inference, communication, and the internal cost of an energy evaluation. They are neither end-to-end operation counts nor measured hardware latency. Alternative parallel SA implementations are outside this comparison.

CPU times are medians over 100 unique nontrivial syndromes at p=0.10 on an AMD EPYC 9754 node, using single-process Python 3.11/NumPy 2.4. SB batches updates, SA performs Python proposals, and matching/BP+OSD use compiled components. Thread counts default to one only when environment variables are unset; the archive does not record their actual values. Single-thread execution is therefore not independently verified.

Figure 4 uses historical dense-search/explicit-SOE timings; Table 2 distinguishes later sparse-search/histogram-SOE timings and archived comparators. Both campaigns use 100 unique nontrivial syndromes per condition at p=0.10; the later campaign reuses syndromes and solver seeds. Optimized times exclude once-per-code support preparation. Search-only excludes seeding and aggregation; end-to-end CP includes both. Raw-log “total” may combine inference evaluations and is not a stage definition. MPS timings use fixed bond dimension, not the targeted-validation REF pipeline.

Accuracy of the fixed-configuration decoder

We first assess the configuration fixed before the held-out evaluation, using the baselines and REF to distinguish practical gains from the remaining accuracy gap. Finite-size behavior and search-budget allocation

The larger-code gap motivates asking whether additional search effort helps and how that effort should be allocated. Increasing the SB step count from 100 to 400 lowers point-estimated LER in all 18 extended-budget cells at distances 11 and 13, with 12 significant after Holm correction. For p≥0.08, REF ratios change from 1.20–1.33 to 1.09–1.12 at distance 11 and from 1.38–1.87 to 1.07–1.23 at distance 13. These are exploratory improvements, not independent confirmation of the selected budget rule.

CP-SB 8×400 and 32×100 use equal flip-energy evaluation counts (4,300,800 at distance 13), not necessarily equal runtime or end-to-end work. At distance 13 and p=0.12, radius-two REF ratios on the 10,000-shot subset are approximately 1.17 and 1.28. The longer run wins/loses 1,935/1,643 discordant shots across 15 cells, with lower LER in nine but only one significant difference after Holm correction. This does not establish a universal allocation advantage.

The distance-11/13 crossing moves from approximately p=0.1167 to 0.1581, versus 0.1765 for REF. Estimates linearly interpolate log-LER differences across p intervals 0.10–0.12, 0.14–0.16, and 0.16–0.18, respectively, without crossing confidence intervals. They demonstrate budget sensitivity, not an asymptotic threshold: sizes are limited and the larger budget was selected after inspecting the scan.

The timing breakdown also shows why fast local counting matters in practice. Discussion

The construction connects low-energy search to degenerate decoding through exact aggregation over a complete prescribed local neighborhood. Exactness does not extend to the full logical-class mass: classes can have different omitted fractions, so local evaluation does not certify their ordering. The held-out and fixed-candidate comparisons support the combination, while larger codes retain a gap to REF.

Both search and mass estimation may contribute to that gap. Matched-work budget comparisons motivate examining run length alongside replica count, without establishing a universally preferred allocation. Similar residual errors under the largest tested SB and SA budgets do not establish convergence or isolate truncation as the cause. Future comparisons should vary search at fixed inference and vary the estimator at fixed candidate pools. Multiple-center or adaptive neighborhoods must avoid duplicate counting and measure the associated overhead.

Histogram counting makes aggregation inexpensive in the tested implementation, subject to the support and overlap conditions of Sec. II E. Its stage-specific speedup is distinct from SB’s work–depth tradeoff. Neither historical CPU timings nor the optimized comparison establishes dominance over the adaptive REF pipeline. Demonstrating hardware benefit requires matched implementations and measurements of end-to-end latency, throughput, memory traffic, and synchronization.

Beyond the surface-code benchmark, applicability depends separately on the coordinate representation and the locality needed for efficient counting. The coordinate construction extends algebraically to binary stabilizer codes with an independent generator basis and 4^(k) logical Pauli classes for k encoded qubits. Small fixed-k families with suitable local bases, including planar color codes, are natural tests; stabilizer-coordinate optimization already exists for color codes . Efficient radius-two counting additionally requires bounded supports and overlap, and the surface-code four-body representation is not general. Local mass depends on the basis; dependent generators can duplicate physical errors. For high-rate qLDPC codes, logical-class enumeration is a further obstacle. This is not yet an efficient general qLDPC decoder.

Circuit-level noise requires a further change from data-qubit errors to fault mechanisms. At circuit level, kernel coordinates of the stacked detector and logical-observable maps preserve both records. Our archived surface-code and bivariate-bicycle checks use the construction associated with Ref. and Stim (Appendix C). Low-weight selected generators still require global completion and do not bound mechanism incidence. A complete decoder must incorporate fault likelihoods, global elements, and nonuniform-weight aggregation; accuracy and runtime remain unmeasured. The missing raw circuit and matrix artifacts also prevent independent reconstruction of the archived structural checks.

Conclusion

We have combined simulated-bifurcation search with probability aggregation over complete prescribed stabilizer-coordinate neighborhoods to connect low-energy optimization to logical-class inference. The coordinates preserve syndrome and logical class without penalty terms, while a support-based histogram evaluates the radius-two mass exactly without enumerating all generator pairs. The resulting local mass remains an approximation to the full logical-class mass. On held-out rotated-surface-code data under independent depolarizing noise, the main decoder improves over the tested matching and correlated BP+OSD baselines, while exploratory increases in the SB step count improve larger-distance accuracy. At fixed candidate pools, local aggregation improves on candidate-MAP inference. In the selected SB–SA comparisons, SB uses more flip-energy evaluations but fewer sequential update rounds; the corresponding hardware latency remains to be measured. These results support a search-then-aggregate approach to code-capacity decoding, with further progress depending on the joint design of search budgets, local mass estimators, and their computational implementation.

 Exact identities and locality conditions

This appendix justifies the feasible coordinates, the flip-energy force, and the radius-two histogram used in Sec. II. It separates exact identities from the locality assumptions needed for efficient evaluation.

Constraint preservation and counting

All binary operations are over 𝔽₂, and Pauli errors are identified up to overall phase. Let H be the syndrome map in the (X ∣ Z) convention, including the symplectic pairing, so that HG^(T) = 0. When the measured checks are the rows of G in the same order, H = GJ, where

$$J=\begin{pmatrix}0&I_n\\I_n&0\end{pmatrix}.$$

A different ordering of measured checks gives the corresponding row permutation of this map. Logical representatives have zero syndrome, so Eq. (2) gives

He_(L)(u)^(T) = He₀^(T) + Hℓ_(L)^(T) + HG^(T)u^(T) = s.

Varying u multiplies the error by a stabilizer and therefore preserves its logical class. Since rank_(𝔽₂)G = m, equality uG = vG implies u = v. A generator-space ball thus maps injectively to physical Pauli errors up to phase. Dependent generators would still preserve the constraints, but could count the same physical error more than once.

For 0 < p < 1, the weight w = p/[3(1 − p)] is positive and finite. For any nonnegative integer radius r, the ball ℬ_(r)(u_(L)^(*)) contains its center, and

$$\begin{gathered}
Z_L-\widehat Z_L^{(r)}
=\sum_{u\notin\mathcal B_r(u_L^*)}w^{E_L(u)}\ge0,
\\ 0<\widehat Z_L^{(r)}\le Z_L.
\end{gathered}$$

This subset-sum bound concerns unnormalized masses. It supplies no bound on the class-dependent fraction of omitted mass and does not certify the ordering of logical classes or calibrated conditional probabilities.

Flip energies and force

With constraint preservation established, we verify how discrete flip energies supply the interaction force used by SB.

Any multilinear energy can be written as E(σ) = A_(j)(σ_( ≠ j)) + σ_(j)b_(j)(σ_( ≠ j)), where neither A_(j) nor b_(j) depends on σ_(j). Flipping spin j gives

Δ_(j) = E(σ^((j))) − E(σ) = −2σ_(j)b_(j).

Since σ_(j)² = 1 at a spin vertex,

$$\frac{\sigma_j}{2}\Delta_j=-b_j
=-\frac{\partial E}{\partial\sigma_j},$$

which proves Eq. (6). The function b_(j) here is distinct from the coefficient bound B_(j) used to normalize the coupling in Sec. II C. The SB update evaluates this identity at σ = sgn (x), with the stated zero-sign convention; it does not identify the interaction force with −∇E(x) at arbitrary continuous positions. This is an identity for the chosen energy, not a theorem of monotonic energy decrease or convergence of the SB updates.

Radius-two histogram

We next derive the counting identity that replaces explicit pair enumeration while preserving the complete radius-two sum.

Fix a logical class and its center u_(L)^(*), and write E₀ = E_(L)(u_(L)^(*)). Let ϵ_(j) be the binary unit vector in generator coordinate j. All changes are measured from the same center:

$$\begin{gathered}
\Delta_j=E_L(u_L^*\oplus\epsilon_j)-E_0,
\\
\Delta_{jk}=E_L(u_L^*\oplus\epsilon_j\oplus\epsilon_k)-E_0
\quad(j<k).
\end{gathered}$$

Write h_(t) = #{j : Δ_(j) = t} for integer t, and define (h * h)_(t) = ∑_(a ∈ ℤ)h_(a)h_(t − a). This convolution counts ordered generator pairs, including self-pairs. Removing the self-pairs and dividing by two gives the histogram of additive changes over all unordered distinct pairs:

$$P_t=\frac12\left[(h*h)_t-\sum_j\mathbf1[t=2\Delta_j]\right].
\tag{A1}\label{eq:A1}$$

Because the Pauli-weight objective is a sum of single-qubit indicators, generators with disjoint physical-qubit supports satisfy Δ_(jk) = Δ_(j) + Δ_(k). Let 𝒪 contain each pair j < k whose physical-qubit supports intersect, including overlap between X and Z components on the same qubit. For each such pair, we remove its additive contribution and insert its true energy change. Including the center and all single flips, the exact radius-two histogram is

$$\begin{gathered}
N_t=\mathbf1[t=0]+h_t+P_t
\\+\sum_{(j,k)\in\mathcal O}
\bigl(\mathbf1[t=\Delta_{jk}]-\mathbf1[t=\Delta_j+\Delta_k]\bigr).
\end{gathered}
\tag{A2}\label{eq:A2}$$

Thus N_(t) ≥ 0 and $\sum_tN_t=1+m+\binom m2$: each overlap correction preserves the total count. With independent generator rows, these counts enumerate distinct physical errors, and

Ẑ_(L)⁽²⁾ = ∑_(t)N_(t)w^(E₀ + t).

The histogram identities are exact in integer arithmetic; the implementation uses fixed-width integer counts and floating-point probability evaluation. Exact counting therefore assumes no integer overflow, while numerical scores remain subject to floating-point rounding and range limits. Exact evaluation of this local sum does not extend to the omitted part of the logical class.

If every generator touches at most a fixed K qubits, single-flip changes lie in [−K, K] and pair changes in [−2K, 2K]. If |𝒪| = O(m), the single-flip counts and overlap corrections require O(m) local work, with fixed-size convolution. Bounded overlap degree is sufficient for this condition. The bound assumes that the physical center and support incidence data have been supplied; it does not cover the complete candidate-selection or decoding pipeline. Dense construction of the overlap matrix, dense reconstruction of candidates, pool scoring, and cache storage are separate costs in the archived code. Even cached-structure lookup constructs a key from the dense generator matrix. The bound also relies on the integer Pauli-weight objective: arbitrary position-dependent likelihood weights require a fresh complexity analysis, and correlated objectives require rechecking disjoint-support additivity.

Structural circuit-level checks

Fault-space coordinates and construction

These checks examine whether fault-space coordinates can preserve detector and hypothesized logical-observable records; they do not evaluate a circuit-level decoder. Let H and L denote the corresponding binary maps, and let A = [H; L] denote their vertical concatenation. All operations are over 𝔽₂. For a fault-mechanism vector f, the records are Hf^(T) and Lf^(T). Generators satisfying AG^(T) = 0 preserve both records under f ↦ f ⊕ uG. A complete independent basis spans ker A, with dimension $N_{\rm mech}-\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_2}A$. Here $N_{\rm mech}$ is the number of fault mechanisms, $H\in\mathbb F_2^{N_{\rm det}\times N_{\rm mech}}$, and $L\in\mathbb F_2^{N_{\rm obs}\times N_{\rm mech}}$, where $N_{\rm det}$ and $N_{\rm obs}$ count detectors and tracked logical observables. If the kernel dimension is M, then $G\in\mathbb F_2^{M\times N_{\rm mech}}$. Here L is a response matrix, not the logical-class label used in the main text. These are fault-space coordinates, rather than the data-qubit stabilizer coordinates of the main benchmark.

The archived instances are memory-Z experiments: rotated surface codes with d rounds at d=3,5,7,9,11,13, and a [[72, 12, 6]] bivariate-bicycle (BB) circuit with six rounds of the depth-eight syndrome-extraction cycle associated with Ref. . The circuit noise parameter is p=0.003. The surface-code circuits use Stim’s generated reset, measurement, post-Clifford depolarization, and pre-round data-depolarization settings . The implemented BB circuit includes post-CNOT two-qubit depolarization, single-qubit depolarization on idle data during gate layers, post-reset and pre-measurement flips, and end-of-cycle data depolarization, with initialization and final readout handled explicitly. The extraction-cycle depth does not include all experiment boundaries. The circuit parameter is not a uniform probability for the merged detector-error mechanisms.

Low-weight candidates are generated heuristically by applying BP+OSD to individual mechanism responses under the stacked map, suppressing the mechanism being reconstructed and rejecting candidates that fail the kernel constraint. Independent candidates are selected over 𝔽₂, and remaining directions are supplied by global null-space completion. The candidate search imposes no fixed spatial or temporal window. The code checks the target rank and kernel membership, but the construction is not a minimum-weight-basis algorithm. Here “global” identifies the completion procedure, not a guarantee that every completion element spans the circuit. Generator weight counts detector-error mechanisms, not physical data-qubit Pauli weight.

Scope and reproducibility limits

No circuit-level decoding LER or online decoder latency was measured in these structural checks. The results motivate a fault-space construction but do not validate likelihood aggregation, search performance, or end-to-end complexity. In particular, nonuniform mechanism weights and global basis elements require treatment before the main search-and-aggregation approach can be assessed at circuit level.

The summary table, distance-13 inspection report, and BB construction report retain the reported structural evidence. The construction script and kernel-basis implementation specify the procedure and its internal checks. The present project snapshot does not include the underlying circuit, detector-error model, or matrix artifacts. Consistency of the retained reports can therefore be checked, but independent recomputation of the archived ranks, kernel membership, and full supports requires recovering or regenerating those artifacts. A new heuristic run need not reproduce the same basis element by element.
